# A first real run, about 45 minutes

Everything built so far has been tested with made-up recordings and a computer voice. This walks through one real job from start to finish, with your real screen, your real voice and a real microphone. Nothing here
changes anything outside this computer. At the end, tell me what you saw (the last section says how).

If a step does not work, do not fight it. Write down what you pressed and what the screen said (or press Windows + Shift + S to take a picture of it), and go on to the next step.

## Before you start (5 minutes)

- **Close anything private.** OpenScreen records your screen, and it makes sounds while it works.
- Open `OPEN EDITOR.bat` and make a new project (any name, for example *First run*).
- Notepad is the program you will use. It has nothing private in it.
- Have this script in front of you, on paper or on your phone. Read it as you do the job. It has the names that were hardest for the computer to hear.

> Welcome to this short lesson. In this video, I will show you how to save a file in Notepad. At our college, we use Brightspace for our courses. First, open the File menu. Next, choose Save As. Then type the name report, and press Enter. The file is now saved. Thank you for watching.

## 1. Record (about 10 minutes)

1. Choose **Record the screen (OpenScreen)...** in the **Insert** menu (it is in the **Narration** menu too).
2. Choose **A whole screen** (or just the Notepad window). Leave your voice and the computer's sound on their defaults.
3. Under **OpenScreen may**, choose **Note where the mouse goes and what is clicked** (or the choice that also notes the keys). Without this, the steps in part 4 have nothing to work from.
4. Press **Start recording**. After the countdown, read the script and do the job in Notepad at the same time: open the **File** menu, choose **Save as**, type *report*, press **Enter**. Go slowly, and click as you say each sentence.
5. Once during it, press **Pause**, wait a few seconds, then **Resume**. Add one marker.
6. Press **Stop**.

Tell me: did anything go wrong (a sound you did not expect, a button that did nothing, a warning)? Did it say it saved the recording?

## 2. Edit it and make a video (about 10 minutes)

1. Press **OpenScreen**, then **Edit the recording I just made**.
2. Press play. Can you jump about by clicking the timeline? Does the picture show your real screen, and do you hear your voice?
3. Try the keyboard: **F6** to move between the parts of the window, the arrow keys to move, **S** to split a clip, **Ctrl+S** to save. Cut away the start and the end if you like.
4. Press **Make a video from this edit**. When it says the video was made, press **Put the video on the current slide**.

Tell me: how does the finished video look and sound? Did the course then ask you for a description and for captions?

## 3. Captions from your voice (about 10 minutes)

This is the newest part, and it has only heard a computer voice and one recording of your narration.

1. With the video on the slide, press **Write captions for the video** (or select the video and press **Write captions...** in Properties).
2. Find *Write the captions by listening to the voice*. Leave the model on **small.en**. Under **Words to listen for**, check that *Brightspace* is there; add them if not.
3. Press **Listen and write the captions**. It takes about a fifth of the length of the recording.
4. Read the captions against the script. Count the words that are wrong. Look at *Notepad*, *report* and *Brightspace* in particular.
5. Play the video. Do the captions appear when you speak?
6. Press **I have checked the captions**, then **Save and use these captions**.
7. For a second try: type or paste the script in **Start from a script**, press **Make captions from this script**, then **Set the times of my captions by the voice**. Do the times now follow your voice?

Tell me: how many words were wrong, which ones, how the timing felt, and whether the wording about "unchecked" was clear.

## 4. Steps and lessons (about 15 minutes)

1. Press **OpenScreen**, then **Make steps from this recording**.
2. Read the list. Are the steps what you did? Did the names of what you clicked come through ("Choose Save as from the menu")? Fix any wording. The typing step should say what you typed.
3. In a step, press **Show the picture**. Is it the right moment? Move the box onto the thing that was clicked, with the arrow keys. Tick the steps you have checked, and write what each picture shows.
4. Choose **Watch** and press **Make or update the lesson: one slide for each step**. Look at the new slides. Then do the same for **Practice** and **Test**.
5. Press **Preview**. Try Practice: press the wrong place three times, then the right one, first with the mouse and then with only Tab and Enter. In the Test, answer one right and one wrong and look at the score.
6. Change a step's words, then make the lesson again. Your own changes to the slides should stay.

Tell me: does the picture show your real screen well? Is the place to click easy to find without the mouse? Does the hint help? Does the score make sense?

## Only if you want to (later)

- **The Accessibility button.** In **Preview**, press **Accessibility** (next to Credits). Try a bigger text size, the dark colours, the reading line, and **Show the transcript**. Tell me whether anything is hard to find or hard to read. (`docs/learner-accessibility.md`)
- **A screen reader** (Narrator: Windows + Ctrl + Enter, or NVDA). Listen to the captions window, the list of steps and a Practice slide. Do you hear what each control is and what it does?
- **Publish** and upload to Brightspace. Does the quiz appear in the gradebook?

## What to send back

A few lines for each part will do. For each, say what worked, what did not, and anything that surprised you. Add a picture (Windows + Shift + S) of anything that looked wrong, and copy any message the screen showed.
The recording is in the `captures` folder of your project; you do not need to send it. It stays on your computer.
