### Screen recorder (OpenStage Capture): getting it into OpenStage

Capture records the screen, edits the recording (trim, split, text, shapes, blur, zoom, webcam, sound, MP4 export) and will make accessible step-by-step and software-simulation lessons. It is Windows first and Apache-2.0. Its own plan, in eight phases, is `docs/capture/roadmap.md` on the `capture-import` branch, and its handover note is `docs/capture/HANDOVER.md`. These are the parts that need doing, in roughly this order.

- [x] **The editing screens in the editor** (built 2026-10-16). The recorder's preview, timeline and clip list open in a dialog from **OpenScreen > Edit a recording**, or **Edit the recording I just made**, with the course's files reached only through narrow shell commands (a recording is named by its folder name, only `edit.json` is ever written, only that recording's videos can be played), the edit saved as `edit.json`, their own F6, undo and announcements kept, and the editor's own keys stepping aside. Tried in the real editor with a made-up recording (a test video and silent sound): the video loads through the shell, and splitting, undo, redo, saving and opening the saved edit work. A dialog of its own, rather than a region of the main window, was my choice: it keeps one set of keys at a time and cannot trap focus. Not yet heard with a screen reader (see below).
- [x] **The recorder panel in the editor** (built 2026-10-15). The toolbar's **OpenScreen** button opens a dialog to choose what to record (a screen, a window, or part of a screen), the microphone, the computer's sound, a webcam and whether to note the mouse and keyboard, then to start, pause, resume, stop, start again and add markers, with every state in words, keys that work from any program, a repair if the recorder dies, and a check that the computer is ready. Recordings go in a `captures` folder inside the course, which is left out of the published course. Tried in the real editor with the recorder's practice source (made-up picture and sound, its own sounds off), not yet with the real screen. Not built: choosing part of a screen by dragging, a list of earlier takes, changing the keys, and putting a recording in a course (the next stage). See `docs/editor.md`. The editor's shell already has what it needs (`stream.rs`, `recorder.ts`): starting the recorder and talking to it while it runs, keys that work in other programs, playing a very large video, and the `OPENSTAGE_CAPTURE` setting.

- [x] **Captions from a recorded voice (D3): trial passed and built, 2026-10-18.** In **Write captions...**, *Write the captions by listening to the voice* downloads a speech model when you press the button (small.en, 488 MB, careful; or base.en, 148 MB, faster; asked first, checksum verified), listens to the clip on this computer with `whisper-cli` (built once by `BUILD SPEECH RECOGNISER.bat`), and writes the captions with the time of every word, leaving out its notes about sounds and anything heard in silence. **Set the times of my captions by the voice** keeps your words and gives them the voice's times. Earlier note: Whisper (`whisper.cpp`, a separate program, with the `small.en` model, 488 MB) heard 99.8% of a known script and 97.8% to 98.9% with laptop noise, agreed word for word with a second model on your own narration, wrote no invented sentences (one "You" on perfect silence), and took about 25 seconds for two minutes. Older note follows. ## Phase 4: Capture module (Windows first)

Screen recording, a mid-level video editor for recordings (trimming, splitting, text overlays and
zoom), and accessible step-by-step and software-simulation output. The recorder is a separate
program (`openstage-capture`, written in Rust) that the editor starts; the editing screens are
TypeScript. The Capture code was brought into this repository on 2026-10-15 (`capture/`, `capture-edit/`,
`capture-timeline/` and `docs/capture/`), and authors see it as OpenScreen. Its own plan, in eight
phases, is `docs/capture/roadmap.md`, and what is left to do is listed under "Screen recorder" at the top
of this roadmap. The reasoning for building our own recorder is `docs/decisions/0001-screen-capture.md`.
What it needs from OpenStage, besides the shell work listed in `docs/capture/openstage-requests.md`:

- [x] Audio description for video in the player (format 0.19.0): an "Audio description" button beside a video that opens an accessible pop-up where the learner can read the description, listen to it, or play the video with the description voiced in time. Shown only where the author has added description. The text is the source and the audio is generated from it, like narration.
- [ ] A free-text input, so a learner can type an answer into a labelled field that is saved in a variable and a rule can check it. (Typed answers already exist as quiz questions: short answer, number and fill in the blanks. This would be an input kind for practice steps that are not questions.)
- [ ] Scoring from steps: hotspot and text-entry steps that are not quiz questions can add points to the score, the results box and SCORM, with step results stored compactly enough for SCORM 1.2's 4096-character suspend data. (Quiz questions already score, with partial credit, and a question worth 0 points is practice.)

Done so far, as format 0.18.0 (no shell or player features needed a decision): a rule that adds to a number variable, so tries can be counted (OS-12); the
hotspot object, defined with a name, a keyboard route and a minimum size (OS-13); provenance that names a capture step, on slides and objects (OS-14); and a
`sourceExclude` list in `project.json` with a warning for raw recording material in the project (OS-15). The editor's shell also now has what the
recorder needs (see "Behind the editor" in docs/editor.md): a way to start the recorder and talk to it while it runs, with a polite stop and heavy jobs
waiting (OS-4); keys that work in other programs (OS-5); playing a very large video from the project (OS-6); and the `OPENSTAGE_CAPTURE` setting (OS-7). The
recorder panel is not built in the editor, and the editing screens are not yet joined to it.

Timing that follows a video or an audio clip is already on the list under Phase 2. Capture works
without it, but with it Capture's step text could be real, accessible objects timed to the video
instead of being drawn into the picture.
